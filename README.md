  <h1 style="color:#145a7a;text-align:center;">Lipids & Cholesterol: Understanding Heart Health</h1>

  <p>
    Lipids are fatty substances found in the blood that play an important role
    in the body's normal functions. Cholesterol is one type of lipid. The body
    needs cholesterol to build cells and produce certain hormones, but
    unhealthy cholesterol levels may increase the risk of heart disease.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color:#145a7a;">What Are Lipids?</h2>
  <p>
    Lipids include cholesterol and triglycerides. They provide energy, support
    cell structure and help the body absorb fat-soluble vitamins. A lipid
    profile blood test measures different types of fats in your blood.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color:#145a7a;">Types of Cholesterol</h2>
  <h3>1. LDL – Low-Density Lipoprotein</h3>
  <p>
    LDL is commonly called bad cholesterol. High LDL levels can contribute
    to plaque buildup in arteries and increase cardiovascular risk.
  </p>

  <h3>2. HDL – High-Density Lipoprotein</h3>
  <p>
    HDL is known as good cholesterol because it helps carry cholesterol
    away from the arteries toward the liver for processing.
  </p>

  <h3>3. Triglycerides</h3>
  <p>
    Triglycerides are a type of fat used by the body for energy. High levels,
    especially alongside low HDL or high LDL, may increase heart-related risk.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color:#145a7a;">Common Causes of Unhealthy Cholesterol</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Eating too many foods high in saturated and trans fats.</li>
    <li>Low physical activity and a sedentary lifestyle.</li>
    <li>Smoking and excessive alcohol consumption.</li>
    <li>Being overweight or having certain metabolic conditions.</li>
    <li>Family history and genetic factors.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2 style="color:#145a7a;">Signs and Symptoms</h2>
  <p>
    High cholesterol usually has no obvious symptoms. Many people discover
    it only through a blood test. Regular screening is therefore important,
    especially for people with risk factors.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color:#145a7a;">How to Maintain Healthy Lipid Levels</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Eat more vegetables, fruits, whole grains and legumes.</li>
    <li>Choose healthier fats, such as those found in nuts and olive oil.</li>
    <li>Exercise regularly, as recommended by your healthcare professional.</li>
    <li>Avoid smoking and limit alcohol.</li>
    <li>Maintain a healthy weight and get regular health checkups.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2 style="color:#145a7a;">Importance of a Lipid Profile Test</h2>
  <p>
    A lipid profile measures total cholesterol, LDL, HDL and triglycerides.
    Your healthcare professional can interpret the results according to
    your age, medical history and overall cardiovascular risk.
  </p>

  <h3>Can high cholesterol be controlled?</h3>
  <p>In many cases, cholesterol can be improved through lifestyle changes and, when necessary, prescribed medication.</p>

  <h3>How often should I get a lipid profile?</h3>
  <p>Testing frequency depends on your age, health history and risk factors. Ask your healthcare professional for advice.</p>

  <h3>Does high cholesterol always cause symptoms?</h3>
  <p>No. High cholesterol generally does not cause noticeable symptoms, which is why blood testing is important.</p>
